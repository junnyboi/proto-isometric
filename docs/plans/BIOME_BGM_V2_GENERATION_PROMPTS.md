# Walker’s Wake Biome BGM v2 — Generation Prompts

These prompts implement the approved acoustic redesign. They intentionally avoid named artists, works, recognizable melodies, and real-language lyrics. All music and vocal material must be original.

## Desert — The Breathing Expanse

Create a 100-second original cinematic desert exploration cue at 74 BPM. Sparse invented-language contralto vocals are allowed only where specified; no intelligible real-language lyrics. Use a dark modal center with an augmented second only as occasional color. Make the sound unmistakably acoustic and human: low bowed cellos and contrabasses, warm violas, dry plucked gut strings, breathy wooden flute, expressive double-reed woodwind, frame drums, brushed skin percussion, and small hand drums. Prominent synthesizers, electronic arpeggios, synthetic brass, synthetic pads, trailer braams, and wall-to-wall percussion must not appear. If sub-bass support is needed, keep it nearly imperceptible beneath the acoustic ensemble.

Build a physical desert into the music: howling crosswinds moving across the stereo field, sand pouring in fine grains, dunes shifting with low granular movement, cloth and wood creaking in gusts, and one distant dust collapse. Treat sand as brushed rhythm and wind as breath between phrases. Keep environmental sounds recognizable but rhythmically placed, with open midrange space for footsteps, impacts, warnings, and interface cues.

The recurring identity is an original three-note descending expedition motif. Voice it first on solo cello, later on double reed, never as a synthetic lead. The vocal performance is a windswept solo contralto or low mezzo using only this wholly invented nonsemantic text: “Aruun na seya… khorai ema… talu shen… oruun ka.” Pronounce it as flowing fantasy phonemes without imitating any real language, existing lyric, or existing melody. Use long melismas, breath, restrained ornament, and one very distant wordless response ensemble. The voice should feel ritualistic, lonely, and monumental, but appear intermittently rather than dominate the cue.

[0:00 - 0:18] Begin with crosswind, fine sand pour, a low cello harmonic, and breathy flute fragments. Introduce the three-note motif slowly on cello. No drums yet. Intensity: 2/10.

[0:18 - 0:40] Dry plucked strings and a soft frame-drum heartbeat enter. A double reed answers the cello. Sand shuffle becomes a subtle rhythmic layer. Intensity: 4/10.

[0:40 - 1:04] The solo contralto enters with “Aruun na seya… khorai ema…” in two spacious calls. Broader low strings support the voice; a distant dust collapse punctuates the second call. Intensity: 5/10.

[1:04 - 1:24] The acoustic ensemble expands: layered cellos, violas, frame drums, wooden flute, and double reed carry an original variation of the motif. The distant response ensemble answers once using only open vowels. Wind remains audible around the instruments. Intensity: 6/10.

[1:24 - 1:40] Pull back to cello, sparse plucked strings, wind, and sand. The contralto closes with “talu shen… oruun ka” on a low unresolved breath. Return to the opening harmony, wind direction, and sparse texture so the ending can connect naturally to the beginning. No fade-out. Intensity: 3/10.

## Oasis / Wetlands — Water Under the Reeds

Instrumental only, no vocals. Create a 100-second original cinematic wetland exploration cue at 70 BPM in a suspended Dorian color. Make the arrangement organic, humid, and close to the earth. Use warm cellos and violas, soft violin harmonics, alto flute, bass clarinet, breathy reed pipes, delicate plucked strings, wooden slit-drum tones, clay-pot hand percussion, and quiet hand drums. Avoid prominent synthesizers, electronic pulses, glossy pads, tropical clichés, cheerful adventure music, and busy lead melodies. Any sub-bass support must be subtle and acoustic-feeling.

Make the wetland itself part of the ensemble: rounded water bubbles rising in small clusters, isolated droplets, reed rustle, shallow splashes, mud suction, submerged resonances, and a very distant soft wet-fauna call. Use bubbles and droplets as pointillistic percussion. Let reeds answer the woodwinds and leave long pockets of air for gameplay sound effects.

The recurring identity is an original three-note descending expedition motif, transformed into a conversational phrase passed between alto flute, bass clarinet, and cello. It should feel luminous, mysterious, fertile, and cautiously welcoming rather than whimsical.

[0:00 - 0:20] Begin with submerged resonance, small bubble clusters, reed rustle, alto-flute breath, and one low cello note. The flute introduces the motif with wide spaces. Intensity: 2/10.

[0:20 - 0:42] Bass clarinet and viola answer the flute. Wooden slit drum and clay-pot taps create a slow organic pulse; droplets land between beats. Intensity: 3/10.

[0:42 - 1:06] Warm cellos and delicate plucked strings broaden the harmony. A shallow splash and mud-suction rhythm lead into a fuller call-and-response between flute and bass clarinet. Intensity: 5/10.

[1:06 - 1:24] The ensemble reaches its gentle peak with layered violas, cellos, soft violin harmonics, reeds, hollow wood, and rising bubble textures. Keep the melody concise and leave room in the center. Intensity: 5/10.

[1:24 - 1:40] Remove drums gradually. Return to submerged resonance, bubbles, reed rustle, alto flute, and one low cello tone. Match the opening harmony and texture for a natural cyclic connection. No fade-out. Intensity: 2/10.

## Frozen Tundra — White Distance

Instrumental only, no vocals. Create a 100-second original cinematic frozen-wilderness exploration cue at 66 BPM in a minor center with spacious open fifths. Make the score acoustic, exposed, and physically cold. Use tremolo violins, natural string harmonics, violas, solo cello, contrabass, bass flute, oboe, sparse wooden or bone-like flute, a snow-muted low drum, soft bass drum, bowed wood, and occasional real metal resonance. Keep bow friction, finger movement, and human breath audible. Avoid synthetic glass pads, electronic arpeggios, holiday associations, sentimental piano, glossy fantasy scoring, and dense percussion.

Build the tundra into the arrangement: howling wind crossing from far left to far right, powdery snow sweep, deep ice groans, distant cracks, small crystalline impacts, and a restrained frozen-metal ring. The wind should breathe with the phrasing rather than form a constant loud noise floor. Ice cracks mark transitions but never sound like close combat impacts.

The recurring identity is an original three-note descending expedition motif stretched into long tones, first on solo cello and later shared by oboe and bass flute. The emotion is vast, brittle, isolated, and quietly determined—endurance rather than triumph.

[0:00 - 0:20] Begin with moving blizzard wind, snow sweep, high string harmonics, bass-flute breath, and a distant ice groan. Solo cello introduces the motif very slowly. Intensity: 2/10.

[0:20 - 0:42] Snow-muted drum and contrabass pulse establish movement. Oboe answers the cello; tremolo violas gather beneath them. A distant crack marks the section change. Intensity: 4/10.

[0:42 - 1:06] Violins widen into fragile harmonics while cello and contrabass continue a restrained human pulse. Bone-like flute and crystalline impacts appear sparingly in the gaps. Intensity: 5/10.

[1:06 - 1:24] The wind strengthens, low strings broaden, and the motif passes between oboe and bass flute over open fifths. Add one deep ice groan and a muted bass-drum accent, but no heroic climax. Intensity: 5/10.

[1:24 - 1:40] Thin back to solo cello, high harmonics, bass-flute breath, snow sweep, and wind returning to the opening direction. Match the opening harmony and atmosphere for a natural cyclic connection. No fade-out. Intensity: 2/10.

## Lava Fields — The Mountain Answers

Create a 100-second original cinematic volcanic exploration cue at 80 BPM. Use an original low male strong-voice choir only where specified, singing invented nonsemantic syllables; no real-language lyrics. Base the piece in a dark Phrygian center with pentatonic-inflected vocal contours. Make the sound heavy, acoustic, physical, and ceremonial: aggressive low cellos and contrabasses, rhythmic violas, contrabassoon, bass clarinet, one piercing reed reserved for peaks, large barrel drums, deep skin drums, stone impacts, suspended gong, iron strikes, and grounded foot stomps. Avoid prominent synthesizers, electronic bass sequences, trailer braams, modern hybrid-score pulses, continuous chanting, and nonstop climax. Any sub-bass support must sit underneath the acoustic low instruments.

Turn volcanic phenomena into orchestrated events: deep distant volcano explosions, fireballs streaking through the stereo field and striking far away, flamethrower-like flame jets used as breathy risers, lava bubbles and bursts, ember showers, rockfall, and subterranean rumble. Keep explosions deep and spatial rather than sharp; environmental effects must add scale without masking gameplay warnings or impacts.

The recurring identity is an original three-note descending expedition motif voiced by low strings and contrabassoon. The choir is a disciplined low male ensemble singing in unison and octaves with grounded chest resonance, precise attacks, antiphonal answers, and ceremonial weight. Use only this invented nonsemantic text: “Khor! Daraan tu! Reng va! Sho varaan! Khor!” Treat these as fantasy syllables with no real meaning. Do not imitate any existing language, lyric, melody, choir arrangement, or soundtrack.

[0:00 - 0:16] Begin with subterranean rumble, lava bubbling, low contrabass bowing, contrabassoon breath, and sparse stone impacts. State the motif once in the lowest strings. Intensity: 3/10.

[0:16 - 0:38] Barrel drums, violas, bass clarinet, and stomps establish a deliberate march. A flame jet rises into one distant volcanic explosion. No choir yet. Intensity: 5/10.

[0:38 - 1:02] The low male choir enters with two disciplined calls: “Khor! Daraan tu!” answered by “Reng va!” Low strings and drums answer each phrase. A fireball passes across the field and impacts distantly between calls. Intensity: 6/10.

[1:02 - 1:24] The strongest passage: choir in unison and octaves sings “Sho varaan! Khor!” over aggressive cellos, contrabasses, barrel drums, gong, stone, and iron. Add a deep volcano blast, ember shower, and a short flame-jet riser. Keep every event rhythmically separated; do not create a wall of sound. Intensity: 7/10.

[1:24 - 1:40] Stop the choir. Reduce to contrabassoon, low strings, lava bubbles, distant rockfall, and subterranean rumble. Return to the opening motif, harmony, and environmental state so the ending connects naturally to the beginning. No fade-out. Intensity: 4/10.
